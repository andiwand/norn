Norn brings every camera in your home into one fast, modern web app. Live video on your phone and desktop, the controls of the camera's own app, and recordings — on a Raspberry Pi, with almost no CPU.

Everything your cameras do, in one app

Watch, control and record every camera in your home from one place, instead of one app per brand.

Live

Live video in the browser over WebRTC, with MSE and HLS as fallbacks. Low latency, on phone and desktop, installable as an app.

Recordings

Plain MP4 files in day folders, continuous or as clips, with thumbnails. Browse and play them in the app, or copy them with any tool.

Triggers

Start a recording with a button, a webhook from Home Assistant or a doorbell, and soon the camera's own motion events.

Full camera control

Pan, tilt, zoom, presets, night vision, spotlight, siren — whatever the camera offers. Each driver describes its controls, and the UI builds itself from them.

No re-encoding

Video is repackaged, not transcoded. FFmpeg only steps in for a camera whose codec a browser can't play. A Raspberry Pi handles a house full of cameras.

A YAML file, no database

Cameras live in config.yaml. Edit it by hand or in the app — Norn keeps your comments and picks up changes while it runs.

One container

The server, the web app, go2rtc and FFmpeg in one image for amd64 and arm64. Or install it from PyPI.

Local first

Runs in your network and talks to nothing outside it. No account, no cloud, no telemetry.

Open from day one

MIT licensed. Adding a camera brand means writing one small Python driver.

How it works

Norn stays out of the video path. It controls the cameras and lets go2rtc move the video.

go2rtc reads each camera once and hands the same stream to every browser and to the recorder, repackaged, not re-encoded.

Norn talks to the cameras for everything else: pan, tilt, zoom, presets and settings. It also runs the recorder and serves the app.

Your browser gets live video straight from go2rtc and everything else from Norn, through one address.

Get started

Docker (recommended)

Save this as compose.yaml and run docker compose up -d. Then open http://<host>:8000. A demo camera is there to try things out.

services:
  norn:
    image: ghcr.io/andiwand/norn:latest
    restart: unless-stopped
    network_mode: host
    environment:
      TZ: Etc/UTC
    volumes:
      - ./config:/config
      - ./recordings:/recordings

Add a camera

Click Add camera in the app, or write it into config/config.yaml:

cameras:
  front-door:
    name: Front door
    driver: rtsp
    url: rtsp://user:pass@192.168.1.20/stream1
    substream: rtsp://user:pass@192.168.1.20/stream2
    record: continuous

Without Docker

With go2rtc and ffmpeg on the PATH:

uvx --from norn-hub norn

Where it's going

  • Live view, recording, clip triggers, demo and RTSP drivers
  • ONVIF: discovery, PTZ, events — and drivers for popular brands, one camera at a time
  • Motion events as triggers, with pre-recording
  • A timeline to scrub through continuous recordings
  • Users and sign-in, for access from outside the home
